# 1. Introduction

AI technologies are increasingly pervasive, in and out of school, and it is changing faster than education systems can easily respond. We believe there is a moral responsibility to teach young people the skills necessary in order to help them navigate the technologies and surrounding issues of their usage. Our role is not to teach young people how to operate particular tools, it is to help them understand how AI systems work, who builds them, and what they mean for people and society. That understanding lets young people make informed choices about the role AI plays in their lives, and gives them the chance to help shape it. 

This document sets out what we mean by AI literacy, the principles that guide how we teach it, and what we would like to see from those who can help make it available to every young person. It is a call to action, and it is also an honest statement of what we are still learning.


## Who we are and how this was developed

We are the AI literacy team at the Raspberry Pi Foundation. Our views stem from our work on Experience AI, a free programme supported by Google DeepMind to help teachers and students learn about AI technologies. The first resources were published in April 2023 for UK secondary educators, and they are freely available anywhere in the world. Working with local partners, we have since translated and adapted the resources and trained educators in over 38 countries. As of February 2026, the resources had been downloaded more than 700,000 times in over 180 countries and were available in 19 languages. Partners had trained more than 30,000 educators, who can reach an estimated 2.9 million young people ([Experience AI impact report](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Experience_AI_Impact_report_2026.pdf)).

What follows draws on original research from the Raspberry Pi Computing Education Research Centre, feedback from educators including an advisory group of teachers, evaluation data gathered with partners, and the experience of partners and teachers in classrooms around the world.


## What this document is, and is not

This is a position statement. It is not a curriculum, a framework or a research report. It sets out our view and our reasons for it, links to the evidence we rely on, and says clearly where we are offering a judgement rather than evidence.

It focuses on AI literacy for school-age learners (K-12). Experience AI's current resources are designed for learners aged 8 to 16, and we say where our principles apply more widely. It reflects our thinking now, and we expect to revise it as the technology, the research and our own experience develop.

It is written for educators, school and system leaders, policy makers, funders, partners and the people who build educational resources.
